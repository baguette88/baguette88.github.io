What Are Funtastic N64 Controllers?

The Funtastic series was Nintendo's line of translucent, colored N64 hardware released between 1998 and 2001. Inspired by the success of the iMac G3's translucent design (and the broader late-90s trend toward see-through electronics), Nintendo introduced vibrant translucent shells for both the N64 console and its controllers.

These controllers use the same internal hardware as standard opaque N64 controllers (model NUS-005) but feature transparent colored plastic shells that let you see the internal circuit board, wiring, and joystick mechanism. They are functionally identical to standard controllers -- the difference is purely aesthetic.

The Funtastic line breathed new life into the N64 in its later years and created a collecting phenomenon that continues to grow. A complete set of all Funtastic colors is a prized achievement among retro gaming collectors.

Did You Know? The Funtastic series was released under different names in different regions. In Japan, they were part of the "Clear" series. Some colors were exclusive to specific regions, making a truly complete worldwide collection particularly challenging.

Complete Funtastic Color List

Below is every Funtastic N64 controller color ever released, organized by release date. Each entry includes the official color name, release regions, rarity, and current estimated value for controllers with tight joysticks.

Ice Blue

Released: 1998 Regions: NA, JP, EU Common

The most iconic and widely available Funtastic color. Ice Blue was the first translucent N64 color released in most markets. Its light blue transparent shell is instantly recognizable and remains the most popular Funtastic color among both players and collectors. Often bundled with the Ice Blue console.

Value (tight stick): $35-$55 | Loose stick: $15-$25

Fire Orange

Released: 1998 Regions: NA, JP, EU Common

A vibrant translucent orange that looks stunning under light. Fire Orange was released alongside the matching console and was one of the original Funtastic colors. It is widely available but commands a slight premium over Ice Blue due to its bold look. A favorite for custom builds with matching orange buttons.

Value (tight stick): $35-$55 | Loose stick: $15-$25

Jungle Green

Released: 1998 Regions: NA, JP, EU Common

A rich translucent green originally bundled with Donkey Kong 64 in many markets. Jungle Green is the most common Funtastic color due to the popularity of the DK64 bundle. Despite being common, it remains a staple of any Funtastic collection and has a devoted following among Donkey Kong fans.

Value (tight stick): $30-$50 | Loose stick: $12-$22

Grape Purple

Released: 1998 Regions: NA, JP, EU Uncommon

An elegant translucent purple that has become increasingly popular with collectors. Grape Purple was produced in smaller quantities than Ice Blue, Fire Orange, and Jungle Green. The purple hue ranges from deep violet to a lighter lavender depending on lighting conditions, making it one of the most visually interesting Funtastic colors.

Value (tight stick): $40-$65 | Loose stick: $18-$30

Smoke Black (Smoke Gray)

Released: 1999 Regions: NA, JP, EU Uncommon

A subtle translucent dark gray/black. Smoke is more understated than other Funtastic colors, appealing to collectors who prefer a refined look. The translucency is less pronounced than brighter colors -- you can see the internals, but the dark tint gives it a sophisticated appearance. Sometimes confused with the standard black controller in photos.

Value (tight stick): $40-$65 | Loose stick: $18-$28

Watermelon Red

Released: 1999 Regions: NA, JP Rare

A beautiful translucent pinkish-red, often described as a watermelon or berry color. Not a pure red -- it has a distinctive pink undertone that makes it unique among N64 controllers. Watermelon Red was produced in significantly lower quantities than the initial Funtastic wave and was not widely available in Europe, making it one of the more sought-after colors.

Value (tight stick): $55-$90 | Loose stick: $25-$40

Banana Yellow (Dandelion)

Released: 2000 Regions: NA (Toys R Us), JP Very Rare

A bright translucent yellow that was a Toys "R" Us exclusive in North America and saw limited release in Japan. The retail exclusivity and late release date (the N64 was nearing end of life) resulted in very low production numbers. Banana Yellow consoles are among the rarest standard N64 variants, and the matching controllers are highly prized.

Value (tight stick): $80-$140 | Loose stick: $40-$65

Ocean Blue (Midnight Blue)

Released: 2000 Regions: JP (limited), AU Very Rare

A deeper, richer blue than Ice Blue. Ocean Blue (sometimes called Midnight Blue) has a darker sapphire tone. It was primarily a Japanese and Australian release with limited distribution. Often confused with Ice Blue in poor lighting or photographs. The key difference is the depth of color -- Ocean Blue is noticeably darker and more saturated.

Value (tight stick): $75-$130 | Loose stick: $35-$60

Extreme Pink (Funtastic Pink)

Released: 2001 Regions: JP, Limited EU Ultra Rare

The rarest standard Funtastic controller color. Extreme Pink was released very late in the N64's lifecycle, primarily in Japan with extremely limited European distribution. A bright, vivid pink translucent shell that stands out even among the colorful Funtastic lineup. Complete in-box examples can command prices over $200.

Value (tight stick): $100-$200+ | Loose stick: $50-$90

How to Identify Authentic Funtastic Controllers

With rising values, counterfeit and reproduction Funtastic shells have appeared on the market. Here is how to verify authenticity:

Shell Quality Indicators

  • Translucency Quality: OEM Funtastic shells have a uniform, high-quality translucency. Reproductions often have inconsistent color density, visible bubbles, or a cloudier appearance.
  • Color Accuracy: Reproduction colors are often slightly off. Ice Blue repos tend to be more teal. Fire Orange repos are often too dark or too bright. Compare against verified reference images.
  • Mold Marks: Authentic shells have specific mold marks and part numbers on the interior. Reproductions typically lack these or have different markings.
  • Plastic Feel: OEM shells have a specific weight and texture. Reproductions often feel lighter or have a different surface texture (too smooth or too rough).

Label and Markings

  • Authentic controllers have the Nintendo logo embossed on the front face
  • Model number NUS-005 is molded into the back shell
  • Serial number sticker on the back (may be missing on very old controllers)
  • Tri-wing screws (not Phillips) on the back panel

Common Counterfeits

  • Aftermarket shells on OEM internals: A real controller board inside a reproduction shell. Check shell quality and markings.
  • Full reproductions: Entirely aftermarket controller sold as OEM. Test button feel, weight, and joystick mechanism.
  • Color swaps: A common color shell (Jungle Green) artificially tinted or dyed to mimic a rare color (Watermelon). Look for uneven color and dye residue.

Collecting Tips and Strategy

Starting a Funtastic Collection

If you are building a complete Funtastic set, start with the common colors first. They are more affordable and will be available when you are ready. Rare colors should be purchased opportunistically when you find a good deal, as supply is limited and prices are climbing.

Condition vs Completeness

Most collectors face a choice: buy loose controllers in excellent condition or boxed controllers in fair condition. For display collections, boxed is typically preferred even in lesser condition. For play collections, prioritize stick tightness and cosmetic condition over packaging.

Investment Potential

Funtastic N64 controllers have proven to be one of the better retro gaming investments. Key factors driving continued appreciation:

  • Fixed, limited supply (no more will ever be manufactured)
  • Growing retro gaming community and nostalgia market
  • YouTube and social media exposure driving awareness
  • Crossover appeal (gaming + design/aesthetic collectors)
  • Console aging reducing the pool of functional controllers

Storage and Preservation

  • Store controllers away from direct sunlight to prevent yellowing and color fading
  • Keep in a cool, dry environment (avoid attics and garages)
  • If displaying, UV-filtering display cases protect against light damage
  • Avoid rubber bands or tight storage that could deform the cable
  • Silica gel packets in storage containers help prevent moisture damage
